The video shows Beacon completing one FSM choreography while the viewer receives world transforms from the project's C++ forward-kinematics runtime. The final frame shows all eight states completed.

Robot

Beacon is a custom URDF robot with eight links: a turning base, an articulated arm, a rotating wrist, two sliding gripper fingers, and a fixed tool-tip link. It has four rotary joints, two prismatic joints, and one fixed joint. The visuals are boxes; the orange marker identifies the tool tip.

This is a kinematic demonstration. The grasp state closes the fingers in free space; there is no object contact, collision simulation, or force control.

Choreography

  1. Home: begin with the gripper open.
  2. Reach: turn the base and extend the arm.
  3. Grasp: close both fingers.
  4. Lift: raise the arm.
  5. Greet left: turn left and rotate the wrist.
  6. Greet right: reverse the greeting.
  7. Release: open the fingers and reset the pose.
  8. Return home: hold the final pose and stop.

Each state publishes a joint-space target. The servo moves toward that target and reports joint positions on /joint_states. The FSM advances after all rotary-joint errors are within 0.018 rad and sliding-joint errors are within 1.5 mm, followed by a 0.7-second hold.

Rendering

The runtime calculates parent-to-child transforms on /tf and composes them into column-major 4 × 4 matrices on /xform_world. A local bridge forwards those matrices to the browser without recalculating forward kinematics.

The Canvas 2D viewer transforms each box's vertices using the received link matrix and its visual offset, projects them into a fixed 3D view, and draws faces in depth order. The orange trail records successive tool-tip positions. The video is a capture of that live canvas, not a separate animation.

Notes

The portfolio additions are separate from the previously submitted C++ FK implementation. No Fetch model or mesh is used.
